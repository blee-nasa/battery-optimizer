BRIEF ABSTRACT

The search for safe, reliable, and compact high-capacity energy storage devices has led to increased interest in all-solid-state battery research. The use of solid electrolytes provides enhanced safety and durability due to their reduced flammability and increased mechanical strength compared to organic liquid electrolytes. Important part of the development of all-solid-state batteries is the design of the cathode composition and microstructure to ensure high performance and electromechanical stability. CathCal (Cathode Calculator) is a web-based calculator for optimizing solid state battery construction. Engineers and researchers input cathode powder component parameters such as electronic and ionic conductivities, grain size, molar mass and density; CathCal returns calculated outputs such as specific capacity, utilization, mass loading for a given powder composition. CathCal can also find the optimal composition in wt% of the powder components and the optimal grain size for each component for achieving best preselected performance metrics, such as highest specific capacity, or maximum utilization, or mass loading. All computation runs client-side without the use of a backend server. CathCal is being developed as part of the Solid Power with Extreme Environment Adaptability and Resilience in Space (SPEARS) project in collaboration between NASA Langley Research Center, NASA Glenn Research Center, and NASA Ames Research Center.


SECTION I – DESCRIPTION OF THE PROBLEM OR OBJECTIVE THAT MOTIVATED THE INNOVATION’S DEVELOPMENT


Solid-state batteries (SSBs) refer to battery systems using solid electrolyte as ion conductors. In comparison to conventional batteries with liquid electrolytes, SSBs often utilize the same battery chemistries. However, the use of solid electrolytes in SSBs has unique advantages with much improved safety, phase stability, structural resilience, and longevity. As such SSBs are expected to overcome key bottlenecks in aerospace energy storage such as safety and extreme environment adaptability. To achieve this, research must be conducted at the level of battery materials and chemistry to optimize the solid electrode and cathode compositions, which are

then used in construction of solid-state battery cell architectures for performance evaluation. It is also necessary to further connect such materials-level studies to scale-up engineering and develop useful cell and battery prototypes toward targeted applications. This technology report focuses on the automation of the selection process of various battery cell components such as solid electrolytes, cathodes, and anodes, and perform design parameter optimization of the cathode powder materials for achieving best performance of the solid-state battery cells. In SSBs the cathode is generally represented as a powder mixture of electrolyte particles, conductor particles, and reactant particles. The electrolyte particles can be lithium-containing ceramic or polymeric grains possessing high diffusivity for Li ions. These particles can vary in size from nanometer to micrometer range. The conductor particles have high conductivity for electrons, and are typically carbon materials of various dimensions. For example, they can be carbon spheres of nanometer dimensions, carbon fibers of high length-to-diameter aspect ratios, or graphene sheets with nanometer holes (so called "holey graphene" developed at NASA LaRC). Power generation at the cathode is produced by the active material particles (e.g., sulfur, lithium nickel manganese colbalt oxide or NMC) that react with the Li ions supplied from the anode through the Li ion-conducting electrolyte particles. An important design objective is for known material properties of each component, electrolyte, conductor, and reactant, to find the optimal composition ratio and particles size to achieve the highest battery performance in terms of maximum material utilization, specific capacity, and high power output.


SECTION II – TECHNICALLY COMPLETE AND EASILY UNDERSTANDABLE DESCRIPTION OF INNOVATION DEVELOPED TO SOLVE THE PROBLEM OR MEET THE OBJECTIVE


CathCal is a web-based calculator for optimizing solid state battery construction. The user, engineer or researcher, input cathode material component parameters such as electronic and ionic conductivities, grain size, molar mass and density. The user can build a small database (of a few dozens of materials) for use in combinations to construct a cathode powder composite. CathCal returns calculated outputs such as specific capacity, utilization, and mass loading of the constructed powder composite. CathCal can also find the optimal composition in wt% of the powder components and the optimal grain size for each component for achieving the highest values of preselected performance metrics, such as highest specific capacity, or maximum utilization, or active material content in the cathode. CathCal calculations are based on an idealized mathematical model, where the cathode powder composite is represented as a random mixture of hard sphere particles with electro-chemical properties specific for each material component, electrolyte, conductor, and reactant. An adjustable parameter can be used for soft particles. Using mathematical equations, derived from the percolation theory and coordination number theory, CathCal predicts and maximizes battery specific capacity by optimizing mass ratios and grain size of each powder component of SSBs cathode composite. The optimization procedure implements a so-called downhill simplex algorithm for numerical optimization in a multidimensional space. The user can assign different weight factors to prioritize certain performance parameters against others, such as maximizing specific capacity vs utilization or component ratios.


SECTION III – UNIQUE OR NOVEL FEATURES OF THE INNOVATION AND THE RESULTS OR BENEFITS OF ITS APPLICATION

This NTR is for a software release. The mathematical model is derived from a known published results in the percolation theory and coordination number theory. The novelty is in applying those results to construct a complete theoretical model of SSBs cathode in a form that can be quickly computed analytically instead of performing long time-consuming simulations. The high computational efficiency of the analytical model allows for performing multiple cathode calculations in real time (in few seconds to a few minutes) in a process of design optimization. The optimization is carried out using a simplified downhill simplex algorithm, which is a well-known method for numerical optimization in a multidimensional design space. The reported software is an improvement in providing a convenient, easy to use, and robust modeling tool for the construction of a cathode powder composite of several types of materials, for the design of optimal high performance SSBs cathode system. The built-in algorithms are simple enough so that all computations run client-side (on the user’s laptop or a tablet with a web-enabled browser) without the use of a backend server.



SECTION IV – SPECULATION REGARDING POTENTIAL COMMERCIAL APPLICATIONS AND POINTS OF CONTACT

The reported technology provides a software tool that can increase the research effectiveness and reduce the cost by generating a guidance for conducting experimental research, particularly for the development of high performance SSBs. By its nature, the method is strictly applicable to SSBs but not battery systems with liquid electrolytes. The software is not meant for commercialization or to be used for developing a commercial product, as it is strictly for basic science research. The limitations of the mathematical model used in the calculations, such as the assumption of hard-sphere particles, uncorrelated random particle distribution, and infinite system size limits, need to be taken into account for the predicted optimized cathode composites. Given these limitations, CathCal should be used only as a guidance or a starting point when building a new cathode composite and should not be considered a final solution.
